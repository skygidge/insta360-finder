## Story 1 — Vuthea Chheang, Alex Apffel & Huy Tran (High Confidence)

**Location**: San Jose State University / Nevis, Caribbean
**Camera**: Insta360 X4
**Published**: arXiv March 20, 2026 / IEEE VR 2026 (pp. 5–9)
**Source**: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.20560

### Summary
Computer science researchers at SJSU's XRI Lab used a consumer Insta360 X4 to create a VR digital twin of at-risk colonial and Indigenous archaeological sites on the Caribbean island of Nevis. They adapted construction Scan-to-BIM photogrammetry workflows for archaeology, finding 5.7K/30fps 360° video optimal for SfM alignment. The resulting work was presented at IEEE VR 2026.

### Key Quote
> "Video Capture Mode (5.7K @ 30fps) with Insta360 X4 video capture became the preferred mode for the final workflow, as the high frame rate ensures that redundant frames are available to eliminate motion blur and guarantee seamless 'Structure from Motion' (SfM) alignment."

### Why It Matters
Proves consumer-grade Insta360 hardware can replace expensive photogrammetry rigs for archaeological site preservation — opening the door for underfunded institutions to document at-risk heritage.

**Camera**: Insta360 X3
